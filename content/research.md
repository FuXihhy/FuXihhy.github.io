Here are some of my ongoing working fields.

### Argyres-Douglas Theory

Argyres-Douglas (AD) theories are four-dimensional $\mathcal{N}=2$ superconformal field theories that provide a setting for studying intrinsically strong coupling. The original example was discovered by [Argyres and Douglas](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9505062) in pure $\mathcal{N}=2$ $SU(3)$ gauge theory, at a special point on the Coulomb branch where mutually nonlocal BPS states become massless simultaneously. The resulting interacting fixed point has no conventional weakly coupled $\mathcal{N}=2$ Lagrangian description.

Broader families can be realized through renormalization-group flows, [class $\mathcal{S}$ constructions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.2270) with irregular punctures, and [type IIB geometric engineering](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.01324). Seiberg-Witten geometry, BPS spectra, and protected quantities such as superconformal indices and [Kontsevich-Soibelman operators](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.7071) offer complementary ways to study their dynamics. Proposed irregular extensions of the AGT correspondence relate supersymmetric partition functions of certain AD theories to [irregular conformal blocks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.1052) in two-dimensional conformal field theory. And one could also check the generalization of the Macdonald index in the AD case.

### Derived Symmetry

Two-dimensional conformal field theory owes much of its power to infinite-dimensional local conformal symmetry. Above two dimensions, Liouville's theorem leaves only a finite-dimensional conformal algebra. [Kapranov's derived conformal algebra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11507) offers a way to revisit this apparent loss: in the complex-analytic setting, the contact geometry of null geodesics packages ordinary conformal symmetries together with deformations of the conformal structure. For flat complex space in dimension greater than two, the infinite-dimensional part reappears in cohomological degree one, suggesting a larger geometric structure behind higher-dimensional conformal field theory.

To give such a structure a physical interpretation, one needs to connect it to an action and its dynamics. Around a classical solution, the [Batalin-Vilkovisky (BV) formalism](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08096) organizes gauge transformations, field fluctuations, linearized equations and their relations into a complex, while nonlinear interactions enter through higher $L_\infty$ brackets. [Homotopy transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07942) connects this description to effective field theory: after specifying gauge fixing and a propagator, eliminating modes produces tree-level interactions among the retained fields. A central question is whether the additional geometric structure can be realized as symmetries of these field-theoretic complexes, and what constraints it could impose on dynamics.
